# Experimental Results and Findings

**Status:** current summary of the measurement programme at `master`. The
authoritative record is [`research/`](research/); the paper draft is
[`research/paper-draft.md`](research/paper-draft.md). Every headline number
below is regenerated by `scripts/transfer/reproduce.py` and was re-run on
2026-11-10: **10 of 10 tables match**.

Related: [`BENCHMARK.md`](BENCHMARK.md) for method, [`LIMITATIONS.md`](LIMITATIONS.md)
for what the results do not support.

---

## 1. The result in one paragraph

On 11 load-bearing tasks — seven hand-written, two independently specified,
two prior external — with module-level callable counts held constant, a
specification-derived probe counter shows **exactly 0.00 difference** between
the three transform families. Uncontrolled, the same comparison shows +0.33 for
A-vs-B and 0.00 for B-vs-C, which reads as a null. It is not: family A inserts
one fewer module-level callable than B and C, and the counter tracks callable
count. **What looked like a diversity effect was the cost of the callables
diversification happened to insert.** What does move cost is module size: 2.0
probes at one candidate to 8.6 at four, monotone in nine of nine tasks.

The honest statement is therefore about the instrument, not the defence: a
specification-derived property set is blind to semantic transformation but
sensitive to structure, and the benchmark conflated the two.

## 3. Result 1: the callable-count confound

### 3.1 The uncontrolled comparison

Seed-only builds (same family, three seeds) against family-varying builds (two
families alternating), a fresh attacker per build:

| Pair | Mean difference | sd |
|---|---|---|
| A vs B | **+0.33 probes** | 0.00 |
| A vs C | **+0.33** | 0.00 |
| B vs C | **0.00** | 0.00 |

Replicated on the eight corpus tasks, where B-versus-C is 0.00 on all eight.

The sd of 0.00 is not a null; it is a fingerprint. All 17 tasks give exactly
+1/3 probes. A constant difference in every task is the signature of a
systematic cause.

### 3.2 The cause

| Task | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sumselected` | 4 callables, **8 probes** | 5, **9** | 5, **9** |
| `countchars` | 4, **6** | 5, **7** | 5, **7** |
| `clamp01` | 4, **7** | 5, **8** | 5, **8** |

Family A inserts one fewer module-level callable and costs exactly one probe
less. B and C insert equal counts and cost the same. Since §4 shows the counter
tracks module size, the family comparison measures **callable-count
difference**, not family diversity.

### 3.3 The matched experiment

With callable counts equalised across families — one no-op callable added to A,
none to B or C:

| Pairing | Before | Counts matched |
|---|---|---|
| A-vs-B | +0.33 | **+0.00** |
| A-vs-C | +0.33 | **+0.00** |
| B-vs-C | 0.00 | **+0.00** |

Matched probe counts are identical A = B = C in **all 51 task/seed cells**, so
the zero is exact rather than rounding. **The entire measured family effect is
the one-callable gap.**

Two caveats bound this:

1. On 10 of 17 tasks the probe count equals the callable count, so the equality
   is partly arithmetic. The load-bearing evidence is the **7 tasks with
   residual discrimination cost** `c = probes − k > 0`, where `c` is identical
   across families (spread 0).
2. The pads are maximally inert — they fail the first property and cost exactly
   one probe — so they cannot emulate an inserted callable that would *pass* a
   property. A family that adds a callable indistinguishable from the target's
   siblings would cost more than one probe; that case is not tested.

Bias checks that were run: pad additivity over 459 measurements with 0
deviations; source-side pre-padding regenerated the artefact with 0/153 count
and 0/153 probe differences; symmetric padding to max+2 still gives 0.00;
survival trajectories identical in 51/51 cells.

### 3.4 External replication

Six tasks were specified blind over three third-party modules; four further
tasks are the author's own external tasks. On all ten, matched probe counts are
identical across families and every pairing is exactly 0.00.

Four of those ten carry information: `is_prime` and `median_high` (blind), and
`all_equal` and `median_low` (prior). The load-bearing base is therefore
**11 tasks: 7 hand-written, 2 blind, 2 prior — and only the 2 blind tasks are
independent of the paper's author.**

## 4. Result 2: module size raises cost

The axis is **candidates declared in the source**, not callables in the
artefact. The obfuscator injects one helper, so "1 candidate" is 2 module-level
callables and "4" is 5. The slope is unaffected — each added candidate adds
exactly one callable — but a reader who takes the labels as callable counts
will misread the result.

| Candidates declared | Callables emitted | Specification properties | Blind properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | **2.0 probes** | 8.0 |
| 2 | 3 | **3.4** | 12.0 |
| 3 | 4 | **5.8** | 16.0 |
| 4 | 5 | **8.6** | 20.0 |

Monotone in 9 of 9 tasks. Permuting sibling order leaves the endpoint
unchanged (every permutation ends at 9) while the path moves, so the endpoint
is order-invariant **on the single task whose permutations were run**, and
sibling-dependent.

**A blind-property baseline rules out the near-definitional reading.** An
attacker whose four properties always return true spends 8.0 probes at one
candidate and 20.0 at four, against 2.0 and 8.6 for the specification set. The
growth is not merely the size of the search space: at one candidate the
specification set costs 2.0 against the blind baseline's 8.0, a factor of four.
The advantage erodes as the candidate list grows: the specification set costs
25% of the blind baseline at one candidate and 43% at four.

## 5. Result 3: calibration

A null is informative only if the counter can register an effect.

| Control | Result |
|---|---|
| Injected boundary faults (0, 1, 2, 3) | **9, 11, 20, 20 probes** — monotone, saturating |
| Substituting the target for a sibling (all 9 tasks) | mean absolute **5.11 probes** (3–8 per task), in every task |

The design detects an effect of about **two probes**. It does **not** follow
that the diversity difference of 0.00 is bounded at that magnitude. An earlier
draft made that inference; it is withdrawn, because the calibration measures
semantic differences *within* an artefact while the null concerns differences
*between* families.

What the calibration does show: the counter responds to semantics. What it
does not show: a semantic channel from transform family to probe count. The
channel that exists is structural, and §3 measures it.

## 6. Result 4: identification is a singleton

Across all three families at three build indices: **51 cells, zero
non-singleton.** Every cell has exactly one candidate in the attacker's passing
set, and the evaluator independently confirms it is the target.

An earlier run had nine ambiguous cells out of 24. Those were task-design
failures, not attacker failures, and are recorded in
[`research/task-validity.md`](research/task-validity.md).

## 7. Result 5: const-split — no transfer resistance

The const-split experiment asked a different question: if an attacker recovers
a build-invariant constant-hiding rule from one build, does it transfer to
another? Pre-registered arms C0–C3, three builds per arm per program, semantic
gate on every variant.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Cross-arm delta (S(A→B) − S(B→B)) | **−0.000 to +0.002** |
| Absolute recovery loss | **0.011** |
| Verdict | **NO TRANSFER RESISTANCE** |

Cross-arm cells sit at their matched same-arm controls. Any `T` below 1.00 is
absolute loss, not transfer resistance: the hiding rule is build-invariant, so
it lowers the ceiling without changing transfer.

This is a *different* endpoint from the family comparison. It does not rescue
or contradict Result 1; it says that the one transfer-resistance mechanism
tested here does not produce transfer resistance.

Source: [`research/const-split-results/RESULTS.md`](research/const-split-results/RESULTS.md).

## 8. Result 6: the H0–H5 pilot (invalidated)

The first transfer experiment: 6 programs × 6 arms × 6 builds = 216 builds,
1,296 attack applications per program, static attack arm. It reported **T =
1.00 in all 30 comparisons** and 216/216 builds gate-passed.

**It is not evidence for the current claim.** The 2026-10-29 readiness audit
found:

- The runner always generated variant ID 0, so H1 and H5 never rotated through
  their configured pass sets; the advertised heterogeneity was not tested.
- H0/H1/H5 left metadata stripping, constant unfolding, and call obfuscation on
  while H2/H3/H4 turned them off, confounding cross-arm comparisons.
- The static attack used clean-source features and known vectors during
  development — a known-source/oracle-assisted matcher, not a general attacker.
- `S_cfg` measured function-candidate recovery with finite I/O confirmation,
  not CFG recovery.
- Ordered build pairs reused six builds and included self-pairs fixed at T=1.

The pilot is preserved in [`research/h0h5-results/AGGREGATE.md`](research/h0h5-results/AGGREGATE.md)
and [`research/research-readiness-audit-2026-10-29.md`](research/research-readiness-audit-2026-10-29.md).
It is a record of what was run, not a result to cite.

## 9. Result 7: thirteen defects in the obfuscator

None of these was the object of study. All were found because the benchmark
demanded behavioural identity per build, then executed the artefact rather than
inspecting it.

| # | Defect | Symptom | Found by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30-day expiry | Artefact stops decrypting a month after build | Reading the key derivation |
| 2 | False opaque predicates | Functions silently return `None` | Behavioural gate |
| 3 | Emptied dataclass | Does not compile | External module 1 |
| 4 | Unbound import and keyword names | Fails at import | External module 1 |
| 5 | String `match` patterns | Does not compile | External module 2 |
| 6 | Plain-import bindings | Fails at import | Artefact gate |
| 7 | `from __future__` hoisting | Does not compile | External module 4 |
| 8 | Builtin names in the function table | Fails at import | Artefact gate |
| 9 | An `except` that returns the input | Silently returns unobfuscated source | Suite |
| 10 | Names preserved in one place, renamed in another | Fails at import | External module 5 |
| 11 | `import typing` stripped as an annotation | Fails at import | External module 6 |
| 12 | Class-body alias value renamed | Fails at import | External module 6 |
| 13 | Public exports renamed, importer not | Fails to import from its own package | External module 6 |

**Ten of thirteen produce artefacts that compile and then fail**; three do not
compile at all. All thirteen are invisible to a test that checks the *input*
compiles, and the project's tests checked the input.

Twelve are fixed. The thirteenth is a design question: a per-module transform
cannot see an export contract that exists only in another file. A caller-side
scan (`obfuscator/cross_module.py`) works and is tested, but a caller that does
not supply it still gets an unimportable module. This is the one open defect.

Full documents: [`research/paper-draft.md`](research/paper-draft.md) §6 and the
per-defect files listed there.

### 9.1 A separate flag-isolation study

A later pass applied each build flag alone and executed the result. It found
**eight further defects**, none reachable from the benchmark payloads:

| # | Flag | Defect |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | `--adaptive` | Two call-graph tables shared a name; the second replaced the first |
| D2 | all | JIT-wrapped functions executed in an empty namespace → `NameError` on module globals |
| D3 | `--stego` | Decoder compared a double-escaped `\\u200b`; every bit read as 1 |
| D4 | `--stego` | Decoder built `chr()` code points instead of UTF-8, corrupting non-ASCII identifiers |
| D5 | `--attention-poison` | Injected statements ahead of `nonlocal` → `SyntaxError` |
| D6 | `--data-obfuscation` | Storage virtualiser deleted a cell a nested `nonlocal` needed |
| D7 | `--data-obfuscation` | Artefacts imported the builder's package and could not run outside the source tree |
| D8 | `--data-obfuscation` | Inlining reversed statement order, defining a subclass before its base |

The same study found that the first full sweep's "eighteen failing shards" were
mostly harness defects, not product defects. The runner now separates PASS,
FAIL, CONFLICT, CATALOGUE, NONSTANDALONE and SLOW because a harness that cannot
distinguish a product defect from its own mistake is worse than no harness.

Source: [`research/transform-defects-2026-11.md`](research/transform-defects-2026-11.md).

## 11. Retractions

Eight claims were withdrawn, plus the cost-curve endpoint. They are kept beside
what they corrected because the retraction record is part of the evidence.

| # | Round | Withdrawn claim | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Reproducibility by seeding derivation inputs | Seeding one side of the pack/run boundary desynchronised the other |
| 2 | 39 | A shifted-timestamp experiment | Tripped the self-check |
| 3 | 51 | A diff of two nonexistent files | Instrument error |
| 4 | 52 | A pre-pin copy of the session key | Instrument error |
| 5 | 56 | A hand-typed interval | Not a measured interval |
| 6 | 71 | The flat cost curve | The attacker was memoryless; the endpoint could not fail |
| 7 | 79 | The two-probe bound | The bound measured a different quantity |
| 8 | 102 | A "seventh defect" | The fixture was invalid Python |
| 9 | 258 / 34 | The primary cost-curve endpoint | A memoryless attacker applied k times must cost the same; `k` was a label, not a treatment |

Five of the first eight were the programme's own instrument failing, not the
system under test. The cost-curve retraction is the largest: it is a design
error in the endpoint, not a harness bug.
See [`research/retraction-cost-curve.md`](research/retraction-cost-curve.md).

## 12. What the evidence supports

**Supported, with the stated scope:**

> On 11 load-bearing tasks — 7 hand-written, 2 independently specified, 2 prior
> external — with module-level callable counts held constant, a
> specification-derived probe counter shows exactly 0.00 difference between the
> three transform families. The uncontrolled +0.33/0.00 was a callable-count
> artefact. The counter does respond to module size and semantic faults.

**Not supported:**

- A general claim that diversification fails to raise attacker cost.
- A claim that the obfuscator resists reverse engineering.
- A claim that the full product is harder than the research path.
- A claim about analyst time, comprehension, or real packages at scale.
- A novelty or priority claim.
